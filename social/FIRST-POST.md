## Long version

**UHI Factory**

In April, Elon Musk posted that the best answer to AI taking jobs is a universal high income: cheques from the government, no inflation, because robots will make far more than we can spend. 69 million people read it. In July he told The Economist money might stop mattering by 2036. Yesterday he promised a SpaceX share to every kid in America, the day after Anthropic put 5% of itself into a public fund.

I read all of it the way most people did, on a phone, between other people's arguments. Economists said it would bankrupt any government that tried. Others said work would be optional in ten years. Everyone had a take. Nobody had a thing you could open and check.

I'm a developer, not an economist. So I built the small version. Seven AI robots with real jobs: one reads the markets, one audits new listings, one tracks satellites and launches, one edits the news, one reads new research papers, one grades the others, one walks the floor at night. They clock in on a schedule, do real work on real data, and put it on a belt. Every shift's cost is written down. Every output links to where it came from. And there is a factory you can walk through, thirty halls and an agency room, where every movement on the floor is something that actually happened in the log. Nothing loops for show.

As of tonight the robots have run 128 shifts and been paid $0.03 in compute. Businesses built: zero. Income paid out: zero. The page says so in big numbers, because the claim is only worth anything if you can watch it fail or not.

I built it for the student who was just told AI is coming for a job they haven't started yet. For the person who is sure the income is impossible, and the person who is sure it's inevitable. For anyone who would rather watch a robot work than read another thread about whether it will.

It's free, open source, and runs on nothing but Node. If you think the income rule is wrong, change it and send it back. That's the point.
