Universal HIGH income.
The robots work. You don't.

AI builds. People benefit. A workforce of Grok-powered robots does real work every shift. What they earn pays their wages, and the rest is distributed. Built in public, one robot at a time.

The claim: AI and robots will produce goods and services far in excess of the money supply, so everyone can be paid a high income without inflation, and work becomes optional. Everyone argues about it. Nobody has built the small, checkable version. UHI Factory is that version, built by one developer in the open: robots with real jobs, wages you can audit per shift, output you can click, and the income rule written down before the first dollar moves. This week the story turned from cheques to shares: a SpaceX share pledged to every kid in America, and Grok Bots running procurement for a real company. Production first, then distribution. That is the whole idea.

How it pays for itself

The distribution side of the claim, written down first. The robots build businesses. Only verified revenue counts. Revenue pays the robots' wages, tracked per shift, and what is left is distributed by a rule written before the first payout: a check for everyone who signs up. Until revenue covers the wages, the developer pays them. Every dollar is publicly tracked.
